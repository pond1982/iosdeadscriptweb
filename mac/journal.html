First prototype through Build 14

The first aisle

The first playable scene gave Elias a typewriter, a dark corridor and a single approaching Slasher. The first key set him walking. The enemy carried its own prompt, so a player looking up to read the next letter also saw the claw coming.

In Build 7, a red fissure interrupted the corridor. Typing The page holds my weight printed a floorboard for each word. Build 9 put an engraved revolver in Elias’s hands. A correct letter fired ink. Finishing a word brought the Golden Ribbon across the cylinder to reload. The recoil grew to twelve poses, and the flash moved behind the barrel so its silhouette held through the shot.

Build 14 saved the next chapter on the last correct letter of the Redactionist fight. The Mac test closed the game at victory, relaunched it and pulled the first cyan proof in the printworks.

Builds 15–28

Five districts, five ways to read

In the Rotting Rotogravure, the printing cylinder stamped two rows of three Deadlines. Their different initials let one key pick a target. The Heavy-Handed Critic brought a proofreader’s correction into the fight. As his shears rose, typing STET told the press to let the line stand.

By Build 27 the wharf’s tide could swallow a word, the bindery could stitch words together, and the tower could erase letters while Elias read them. The next build added six named passages to each chapter. Between fights, Elias sorts stamped sheets, signals a lighthouse and salvages signed originals.

Builds 29–31

Elias’s office

A property slip arrives under the office door. A photograph is missing. Elias types a sample on his own machine so he can compare its marks with the clerk’s carbon copy. This became the story behind the eight lesson files and their 33 touch-typing sheets.

The finger guides first appeared as ink-stamped hands. Build 31 brought the view down over the typewriter. A key dips, a typebar strikes and a ghost letter takes ink. At the edge of the line the carriage returns, carrying the player’s work up the page. Try the typewriter in your browser.

Builds 32–35

The voice of Oakhaven

Seventeen films carry the story from the archive steps through the five districts. Elias narrates all 36 shots. Each shot waits for him to finish speaking, and the score recedes under his voice. The narration, music and effects travel with the game so the entire story plays offline.

The title screen returned Elias to his lamplit office. Its typeface, Special Elite, comes from the original manuscript interface. It also sets the four words on the cover.

“words are like bullets”

Builds 36–37 · Mac version 1.0

The clerk’s proof

In the first desk-to-aisle playtest build, finishing the home-key lines took the player away from the typewriter. A separate screen waited for another press of Return before the clerk’s proof would open.

Build 37 keeps the player at the desk. The carbon rolls out and the clerk’s proof feeds in. A line crosses the stray lowercase e, and the Delete key stays lit until the correction lands. The local report records the wait before the first keystroke and the time to a successful Delete. The paper’s own travel takes no time off the player’s clock.

The Mac App Store submission used this build for version 1.0. It runs on Intel and Apple silicon and keeps campaign bookmarks, lessons and optional playtest reports on the Mac.
